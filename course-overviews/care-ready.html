Care Ready™

Safety and Care Essentials for Nannies and Professional Caregivers

A 150-minute instructor-led workshop for adults who care for other people's children

What it is

Care Ready is a hands-on workshop for nannies, au pairs, household caregivers, postpartum helpers, and any adult paid or trusted to care for infants and children in a family's home. It assumes competence and fills the gaps. Most working caregivers can settle a toddler and run a nap schedule. What they usually have not had is training for the low-frequency, high-stakes moments: a choking infant, an unresponsive child, an allergic reaction, a head injury on the playground.

What is covered

The professional caregiver role, and the four agreements to settle in writing Ages and stages, and what supervision has to look like at each one Behavior tools that work without punishment Safe sleep, feeding, choking-risk foods, allergies, and medicationInjury prevention and a first-day safety sweep of an unfamiliar home Deciding fast between 9-1-1, calling the parent, and handling it yourself Choking rescue for infants and children, practiced on manikins CPR for infants and children, practiced on manikins

What participants leave with

A Care Ready Handbook to keep, with fill-in pages for emergency information, allergies, medication, a first-day safety sweep, and questions to bring to the family.

A certificate of completion

Practiced hands on infant and child CPR and choking rescue

One thing to be clear about This is a skills course, not a certification course. Participants learn and practice CPR and choking rescue and are able to perform them, but they do not leave holding an American Heart Association or Red Cross CPR card. If a family requires a card, we will point you to the certifying courses in this area.

Where we teach it

Anywhere in the DC, Maryland, and Virginia area. A family's home, an agency office, a preschool or synagogue classroom, or a community room. We bring the manikins, the handbooks, and everything else.

Who it works for

Nanny agencies onboarding new placements or offering continuing education

Families hiring a first nanny or au pair, often paid for by the family

Nanny groups and parent associations organizing a session together

Postpartum doula and newborn care agencies

Preschools and religious schools serving the caregivers in their community
