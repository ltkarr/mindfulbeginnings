About the club

Little Medic teaches young children the first steps of first aid in a way that is calm, hands-on, and fun. Every meeting follows the same six-part rhythm: a check-in circle, a short story with a stuffed-animal patient, the week’s big steps taught with a chant and hand motions, practice stations, a movement game, and badge time. Members bring their own stuffed-animal patient and bandage, cool, and care for it all season.

The whole club rests on three habits a five-year-old can master: stay calm, get a grown-up, and help with kind hands. Little Medics learn what a real emergency is and how to call 9-1-1, how to tell a grown-up clearly what is wrong, how to care for a scrape or a bump, how to wash hands the right way, how to stay safe around hot things, and how to recognize choking and shout for help. Little Medics are never taught to treat a big hurt alone or to perform choking rescue or CPR. Their job is to notice, tell, and get help.

Week by week

The club runs eight weeks, one hour a week. Two optional extras, Weather Ready and Graduation, can lengthen the season, and any week can stretch or repeat: sites adapt the season as they see fit.

Week 1: Welcome, Little Medics!. Build club culture, learn what a medic does, take the Little Medic Pledge, and meet Rule #1.

Week 2: Tell It! Calm Bodies, Clear Words. How to calm your body and tell a grown-up exactly what is wrong: where, what, and how much.

Week 3: 9-1-1 Heroes. When a hurt is a real emergency, how to call 9-1-1, and what to say when the helper answers.

Week 4: Boo-Boo Basics. Scrapes and small cuts: the four steps, gloves and germs, and real bandage practice on a patient.

Week 5: Bumps, Bruises, and Nosebleeds. What to do for a bump or bruise, the nosebleed steps, and why a head bump always means a grown-up.

Week 6: Hot Stuff! Burns and Fire Safety. What to do for a small burn, how to stay away from hot things, and the fire safety moves every child needs.

Week 7: Choking and Safe Eating. The signs of choking, what a Little Medic does (shout and stay), and the safe eating rules that stop choking before it starts.

Week 8: Bites, Stings, and Itchy Things. Bee stings, bug bites, poison ivy, pet safety, and the allergy signs that mean a grown-up right now.

Optional extra: Weather Ready. Staying safe outside in the sun, heat, cold, and storms, plus the water rule.

Optional extra: Challenge Day and Graduation. Station review of every skill, the Little Medic Challenge, certificates, and a celebration.

What makes it special

  • A real skill set, taught at a pace that fits five-, six-, and seven-year-olds.
  • The stuffed-animal patient. Every week it comes home bandaged and loved.
  • Handbook pages marked Finish at Home that turn the club into a family conversation: the My Address Card, the family meeting spot, and the safe eating rules.
  • A graduation that feels real: certificate, badge, and applause.
